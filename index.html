Private Lending Brisbane Before You Start Explained

When preparing for private lending brisbane before you sending enquiries, settle a short list of facts that any third-party or non-bank funder will need in order to price risk. The broader overview of private lending brisbane describes funding that is commercial and business-purpose only, so the first filter is whether your need qualifies at all.

  • The asset: the property type, its location, and a realistic view of what it could be valued at.
  • The purpose: a business or commercial requirement, because owner-occupied consumer home lending sits outside this category entirely.
  • The position sought: senior debt, a bridging gap, a mezzanine layer, a second mortgage, a caveat-style requirement or development funding. These are not interchangeable.
  • The exit: whether repayment comes from sales, refinance or completion, and the date each is expected.

Each element narrows the structure. An enquiry that names the security, the purpose and the repayment path can be assessed. One that does not tends to stall at the first question, because a lender cannot price risk from a vague request.

Six funding categories, six different conversations

The published service mix for Brisbane borrowers covers six categories. Knowing which one matches your situation shapes what you prepare and what the funder examines first.

  • Private lending: general commercial capital from non-bank and private lenders, secured against property or other assets. The conversation starts with security value, borrower purpose, amount required and exit.
  • Construction loans: narrower, supporting a build in progress with draws released against the construction program as work is completed.
  • Bridging finance: covers a short timing gap, such as capital to settle a site before another sale completes.
  • Mezzanine finance: a subordinated layer within the capital stack, sitting behind senior debt.
  • Development finance: funding tied to a development sequence, where presales or a takeout strategy matter.
  • Residual stock loans: releasing equity tied up in completed but unsold stock after practical completion.

Confusing these categories produces mismatched enquiries. Refinancing residual stock after practical completion is a different question from funding a build in progress, and both differ from bridging capital used to settle before a sale.

Security types that shape the risk discussion

Brisbane presents very different property-backed scenarios, and the security type changes the funding question. The security types described span residential, commercial, industrial, specialised, childcare and agribusiness assets. A townhouse project, a commercial acquisition, a childcare site, an industrial asset and a rural security each ask something different of a lender.

The common thread is whether the security, the borrower purpose and the repayment path make sense together for a third-party private or non-bank lender. Before focusing on rate, ask what documents the funder needs, what assumptions sit behind the valuation, what conditions must be satisfied before settlement, and what happens if sales, refinance or completion take longer than expected.

Price the deal against equity, time and risk

Headline price alone is a weak test. The framing used across the broader guide to private lending in Brisbane values deals through equity protection, return on equity, time, risk and the relationship needed for the next transaction. Compare the cost of capital with the return on equity and the cost of delay.

When assessing a proposed structure, make each assumption visible: which lender type is being approached, whether the loan is senior or subordinated, what security is required, what fees and conditions apply, and which events could change the offer before settlement.

Who this applies to

This preparation guide applies to borrowers whose requirement is commercial or business-purpose and secured by property or another asset that can be assessed. The most relevant readers are developers, asset-backed business borrowers and property owners weighing non-bank funding against the opportunity cost of waiting for bank-style approval.

  • Developers comparing land, build and cost-to-completion funding.
  • Owners of completed but unsold stock looking to release equity after practical completion.
  • Commercial borrowers using residential, commercial, industrial, specialised, childcare or agribusiness-backed security.

It does not apply to consumer credit or owner-occupied home loan advice. ASIC Moneysmart is a better starting point for general consumer home loan education.

  1. Confirm the purpose qualifies. Check the need is commercial or business-purpose, because consumer and owner-occupied home lending is outside this funding category.
  2. Document the asset and amount. Set out the property details, the amount sought and the security position you are asking for.
  3. Set the timeline and exit. State how repayment will occur, whether through sales, refinance or completion, with expected dates.
  4. Compare total cost, not rate. Weigh the cost of capital against the return on equity and the cost of delay before accepting any structure.
  5. Stress test the assumptions. Ask what happens if the exit is delayed, and whether a cheaper option would increase timing risk.

Common questions

Is this kind of funding suitable for an owner-occupied home loan? No. The categories described here are commercial and business-purpose only, secured by property or another assessable asset. For consumer home loan education, ASIC Moneysmart is a better starting point than any private capital discussion.

What should I prepare before asking for indicative terms? Send the project, the numbers and the timeline. Be ready to identify the property, the purpose of the funds, the security position, the amount sought and how repayment is expected to occur, along with whether you need senior debt, a bridging gap, a mezzanine layer, a second mortgage, a caveat-style requirement or development funding.

Should I simply accept the lowest quoted rate? Not without understanding total cost and practical conditions. Ask which lender type is approached, what fees and conditions apply, what could change the offer before settlement, and how the structure protects equity. A useful test under pressure is that no deal is better than a bad deal: funding that solves an immediate problem but damages the next transaction is rarely cheap.
